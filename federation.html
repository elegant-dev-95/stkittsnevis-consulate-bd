Discover Saint Kitts and Nevis

A beautiful twin-island nation in the Eastern Caribbean, Saint Kitts and Nevis is known for its rich history, vibrant culture, natural beauty and warm hospitality. It is the smallest sovereign federation in the world and the smallest country in the Western Hemisphere by both area and population.

Official NameFederation of Saint Kitts and Nevis
CapitalBasseterre (Saint Kitts)
Independence19 September 1983
Official LanguageEnglish
CurrencyEastern Caribbean Dollar (XCD)
MottoCountry Above Self
GovernmentFederal parliamentary constitutional monarchy
Head of StateKing Charles III (represented by a Governor-General)

The islands were among the first in the Caribbean to be colonised by Europeans. Saint Kitts is often called the “Mother Colony of the West Indies.” After centuries of British and French presence, the federation achieved independence in 1983 and remains a Commonwealth realm.

Diplomatic relations with Bangladesh were established on 31 August 2020. Both countries share membership in the Commonwealth and collaborate on multilateral issues.

Location & Geography

Twin islands in the Eastern Caribbean

Saint Kitts and Nevis lies in the Caribbean Sea, about one-third of the way from Puerto Rico to Trinidad and Tobago. The two islands are separated by a narrow channel known as The Narrows, only about 3 km wide.

The islands are of volcanic origin. Saint Kitts is dominated by the dormant volcano Mount Liamuiga, while Nevis features the volcanic peak of Nevis Peak. The landscape includes rainforests, coastal plains and beautiful beaches.

Culture & People

A vibrant Caribbean heritage

The people of Saint Kitts and Nevis are known as Kittitians and Nevisians. English is the official language, and a local English-based Creole is widely spoken.

  • Culture — Rich in African, British and Caribbean influences. Music, dance, festivals and cuisine reflect this blend. Cricket is a shared passion, as in many Commonwealth nations.
  • Festivals — The annual St. Kitts Music Festival and cultural events around Independence Day (19 September) are major highlights. Carnival traditions are also celebrated.
  • Cuisine — Features fresh seafood, tropical fruits, and local dishes such as stewed saltfish, goat water (a traditional soup), and coconut-based recipes.
  • Heritage — Sites like Brimstone Hill Fortress and the birthplace of Alexander Hamilton on Nevis connect the islands to broader Caribbean and world history.

The national motto “Country Above Self” reflects the spirit of unity and service that characterises the federation.
