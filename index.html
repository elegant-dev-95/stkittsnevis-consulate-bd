The Consulate

A bridge between two nations

The Honorary Consulate of Saint Kitts and Nevis in Bangladesh serves as a point of contact, promoting awareness of the Federation and supporting closer diplomatic, cultural and commercial relations between our two countries.

Established in January 2024, the consulate assists members of the public, businesses and visitors with general information and directs official enquiries to the appropriate authorities of Saint Kitts and Nevis.

Bangladesh and Saint Kitts and Nevis established formal diplomatic relations on 31 August 2020 through a Joint Communiqué signed in Washington, D.C. Both countries share a history of Commonwealth membership and collaborate on multilateral issues.

Honorary Consul

Mr. Tanveer Ahmed

Mr. Tanveer Ahmed serves as the Honorary Consul of the Federation of Saint Kitts and Nevis in Bangladesh.

In this capacity, he works to strengthen friendship and cooperation between Saint Kitts and Nevis and Bangladesh. He supports closer diplomatic engagement and encourages connections in trade and investment, tourism, cultural exchange, and people-to-people ties.

The Honorary Consul has hosted receptions marking the Independence Day of Saint Kitts and Nevis, bringing together members of the diplomatic community, government officials, business leaders and distinguished guests to celebrate the bilateral relationship.

The consulate was established in January 2024. For official consular matters, enquiries are directed to the appropriate authorities of Saint Kitts and Nevis.

The Federation

Discover Saint Kitts and Nevis

A beautiful twin-island nation in the Eastern Caribbean, Saint Kitts and Nevis is known for its rich history, vibrant culture, natural beauty and warm hospitality. It is the smallest sovereign federation in the world and the smallest country in the Western Hemisphere by both area and population.

Official NameFederation of Saint Kitts and Nevis
CapitalBasseterre (Saint Kitts)
Independence19 September 1983
Official LanguageEnglish
CurrencyEastern Caribbean Dollar (XCD)
MottoCountry Above Self

The islands were among the first in the Caribbean to be colonised by Europeans. Saint Kitts is often called the “Mother Colony of the West Indies.” After centuries of British and French presence, the federation achieved independence in 1983 and remains a Commonwealth realm.

Diplomatic relations with Bangladesh were established on 31 August 2020. Both countries share membership in the Commonwealth and collaborate on multilateral issues.

Highlights

A twin-island nation of history and beauty

Saint Kitts and Nevis offers a rich blend of heritage, natural scenery and Caribbean culture. The federation is renowned for its well-preserved history, lush landscapes, and warm people.

Historic & Cultural Sites

  • Brimstone Hill Fortress National Park — A UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the best-preserved 17th–18th century fortifications in the Caribbean. Built primarily by enslaved Africans, it is often called the “Gibraltar of the West Indies.”
  • Basseterre — The capital of Saint Kitts, featuring the historic Circus with the Berkeley Memorial clock, Independence Square, Georgian architecture, and the National Museum.
  • Charlestown, Nevis — The charming capital of Nevis, known for its Georgian buildings and as the birthplace of Alexander Hamilton. The Museum of Nevis History is located in the house where he was born.
  • St. Kitts Scenic Railway — The last railway in the West Indies, originally built to transport sugar cane and now offering scenic tours through the island’s countryside.

Natural Beauty

  • Mount Liamuiga — A dormant volcano and the highest point on Saint Kitts. Hiking through its rainforest and cloud forest leads to a dramatic crater and panoramic views.
  • Beaches — Pinney’s Beach on Nevis and the beaches of Frigate Bay and South Friar’s Bay on Saint Kitts.
  • Lush rainforests, volcanic landscapes, and clear Caribbean waters ideal for snorkelling and diving.

The economy is primarily driven by tourism, with additional activity in financial services and light manufacturing. Sugar production, once the mainstay, ended in 2005. The islands are known for their hospitality and the motto Country Above Self.
